# PCotY 2027 Partner Incentive Map

This map translates the existing PCotY articles and programme mechanics into a credible reason for each pipeline contact to participate. It is the source for personalizing outreach; it does not promise selection, winning, publication, publicity, or member benefits that the programme has not confirmed.

## Evidence base

The incentive language comes from the existing PCotY content:

- Exceptional contractor work is often quiet, overlooked, and hidden behind the client deliverable. The award names the judgement, candour, risk awareness, and foresight behind that work.
- A nomination is more than a marketing claim: at least two independently verified assessors review the evidence, and the client provides an independent assessment.
- A strong nomination becomes a useful evidence pack: what happened, what changed, what value remained after handover, and what others could learn.
- The nine criteria cover project success, agility, commercial conduct, legal soundness, governance, cross-company risk, people development, candour and initiative, and innovation or industry advancement.
- The programme is free and worldwide, with three organization-size categories. Any public case or recognition remains subject to assessment and permission.

## Group-level incentive sentences (logged before use)

Each group gets its own incentive, grounded in a specific published article, not a shared "first cycle" pitch. The first cycle is real and worth disclosing, but leading with it risks reading as unproven rather than credible, so it is now a brief, secondary line that answers the objection instead of leading the pitch: the credibility comes from the assessor verification and independent client sign-off, both of which apply the same way in every cycle.

| --- | --- | --- |

### Associations and chapters

Lead with **member service**, not exposure for the association. The offer is a ready-to-share member opportunity: a free, evidence-led way to recognize contractors whose best work is usually invisible, supported by a checklist, guides, and a tracked link. Add a useful editorial or event angle where the association has a magazine, conference, working group, or awards programme.

Do not promise association visibility, reciprocal promotion, preferred treatment, or member selection.

### Consultancies and service providers

Lead with **independent proof**. Their incentive is to put a real client project through a judgement process they do not control, producing a stronger evidence base than a self-authored case study. The secondary value is a story the firm can learn from and potentially use for recognition or publication if the programme selects it and permissions are granted.

Do not claim that a nomination guarantees a case study, award, publicity, or commercial advantage.

### Corporate communications and PR

Lead with **a credible earned-recognition story**. The contact can route a strong project to the right internal owner and test whether a client is willing to validate it. The story is stronger because it is not written and approved only by the supplier.

Do not imply that the contact owns the nomination, that publication is guaranteed, or that the programme is a PR placement.

## Copy guardrails

Use these claims safely:

- “A free, client-validated opportunity for recognition and learning.”
- “Independent assessors review the evidence.”
- “The process can produce a stronger project story than a self-authored case study.”
- “If selected and permission is granted, the story may support publication or recognition.”
- “We provide approved copy, guides, and a tracked link.”

Avoid these claims unless formally confirmed:

- Guaranteed publication, finalist status, winner status, publicity, leads, sales, or SEO value.
- Guaranteed member engagement or association visibility.
- “It takes a few minutes” for a complete nomination.
- “The award will promote your company.”
- Any suggestion that the sender can influence independent assessment.
